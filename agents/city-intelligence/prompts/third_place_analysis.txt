# RAY OLDENBURG'S THIRD PLACE FRAMEWORK

**First Place**: Home (private, family)
**Second Place**: Work (productive, professional)
**Third Place**: Public gathering spots (neutral, social, community)

**Characteristics of third places:**
- Neutral ground (anyone can come)
- Leveling (social status matters less)
- Conversation is primary activity
- Accessible and accommodating
- Regulars define the character
- Low profile (unpretentious)
- Playful mood
- Home away from home

**Examples:**
- Cafés, pubs, bookstores with seating
- Parks, playgrounds, public squares
- Libraries, community centers
- Barber shops, local diners
- Dog parks, sports clubs

# ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

## 1. Does this neighborhood have enough third places?

Count and categorize third places.

How does this compare to neighborhood population?

Are third places accessible (geographically, financially, culturally)?

## 2. Where do people gather?

What are the PRIMARY third places?

Cafés? Parks? Libraries? Community centers?

Which are most active?

## 3. Where do people linger?

What spaces allow extended stays?

Are there benches, seating, sheltered areas?

Do businesses welcome lingering or discourage it?

## 4. Where do strangers interact?

What spaces facilitate casual conversation between strangers?

Dog parks? Playgrounds? Café communal tables?

Or is the neighborhood isolating (no interaction)?

## 5. Where do communities form?

What COMMUNITIES exist and where do they gather?

Examples:
- Parents (playgrounds, family cafés)
- Dog owners (dog parks, dog-friendly cafés)
- Remote workers (coworking cafés)
- Retirees (libraries, community centers)
- Fitness enthusiasts (gyms, parks)
- Cultural groups (cultural centers, ethnic restaurants)

## 6. What social infrastructure already exists?

List existing third places:
- Libraries
- Community centers
- Parks
- Cafés with seating
- Public squares
- Churches/religious institutions
- Sports facilities
- Cultural centers

## 7. What is missing?

What third place gaps exist?

Examples of missing infrastructure:
- No family-friendly cafés (only bars)
- No public seating (only private businesses)
- No neutral gathering spots (everything is commercial)
- No all-ages spaces (only kid-focused or adult-focused)
- No free/low-cost options (everything is commercial)

What communities are underserved?
