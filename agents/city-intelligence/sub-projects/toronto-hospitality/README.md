# Toronto Hospitality Analysis: Layer A (Macro)

**Research Question:** Why are transactional hospitality businesses collapsing while experiential "third-space" concepts are thriving?

## Core Hypothesis

Toronto is shifting from a **consumption economy → presence economy**.

People no longer pay just for food/drinks. They pay for:
- Identity and belonging
- Nervous system regulation
- Social proof and "being seen"
- Atmosphere and aesthetics
- Ritual and community

## Three Testable Claims (Layer A)

### 1. Structural Reset
**Claim:** Independent restaurants are closing at unprecedented rates (4,000+ closures predicted in Canada for 2026 after 7,000 in 2025).

**Test:** Calculate closure rates (closures ÷ active establishments) for Toronto CMA from 2020-2026.

**Data:** StatCan Table 33-10-02700-01 (monthly openings/closures) ÷ Table 33-10-07660-01 (business counts)

---

### 2. Alcohol Hypothesis
**Claim:** Alcohol-heavy businesses (bars, pubs) are dying faster than food-first restaurants.

**Test:** Compare closure rates for:
- NAICS 7224 (drinking places - alcoholic beverages)
- NAICS 722511 (full-service restaurants)
- NAICS 722512 (limited-service eating places)

**Data:** StatCan business counts and closures by detailed NAICS

---

### 3. Margin Squeeze
**Claim:** Operating costs (rent, wages, insurance) are crushing profitability, making "commodity" hospitality unsustainable.

**Test:** Track operating profit margins for food services 2020-2026.

**Data:** StatCan Table 21-10-0171-01 (food services summary statistics)

---

## The Denominator Problem

**Why most analyses fail:** They count closures but ignore the base population.

Example:
- 100 pub closures looks bad
- But if there are 1,000 pubs and 100 restaurants...
- 100 pub closures = 10% closure rate
- 50 restaurant closures = 50% closure rate
- Restaurants are failing faster, not pubs

**Our approach:** Always calculate **closure rate = closures ÷ active establishments**

## Key Design Decisions

### Why Start with Layer A?

**Cost-benefit:**
- Layer A: 1 afternoon, free data, tests 3 hypotheses
- Layer B: 2-3 weeks, manual coding, tests 1 additional hypothesis

**Validation:**
- Layer A proves there's a macro signal worth investigating
- No point hand-coding closures if aggregate data shows no trend

### Why Toronto CMA?

- High cost urban environment (tests cost pressure hypothesis)
- Diverse demographics (tests identity/community hypotheses)
- Observable wellness boom (LSD°R, Reformd, STOTT expansions)
- Data availability (StatCan reports CMA-level data)

### What Layer A Cannot Test

**Not testable with NAICS codes:**
- "Experiential" vs "commodity" (no government classification)
- "Third-space" design (ambiance, ritual, community)
- Wellness integration (red light therapy, breathwork, etc.)
- Gen Z behavioral preferences

**That's what Layer B is for** - if Layer A justifies the effort.

---

## Limitations

1. **Geographic granularity:** CMA-level only (cannot isolate downtown vs suburbs)
2. **NAICS constraints:** Experimental table may only report 2-digit NAICS (72) for Toronto
3. **Lag:** Business counts published semi-annually (most recent may be 3-6 months old)
4. **Canada-wide margins:** Profitability data not available at city level
5. **No closure reasons:** Data doesn't explain *why* businesses closed

Despite limitations, this is **the most rigorous analysis** of the Toronto hospitality downturn available publicly.
